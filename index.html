How OddsEngine works.

An engine that forecasts the daily high in about 50 cities and compares it with the prices on Polymarket. When the market looks off, it makes a call. Every result gets posted, hit or miss.

On top of that: 460+ AI models in one place, paid per use with $ODDS, for people and for AI agents.

01What it is

Polymarket runs a market every day on how hot it gets in cities like Paris, Seoul, Chicago or Madrid. Each market is split into temperature fields: 17°C, 18°C, 19°C and so on. Whatever the official weather station reports at the end of the day wins.

OddsEngine reads several weather models for the exact station the market uses, turns them into a probability for every field and compares that with the price. Most markets are priced about right, so most of the time the engine stays quiet. When the price is clearly lower than what the engine expects, it makes a call.

That is the whole product. It does the math, it posts the call before the station reports, and it scores itself in public.

02How to read a call

ODDSENGINE · MADRID · READ Block: 19°C · 20°C · 21°C Market price · 77c (the market says 77%) Weather model · 82% Honest odds · 77% 👀 READ ONLY · no bet
  • Block: the fields the engine picks together. If the official high lands in any of them, the call hits.
  • Market price: what the whole block costs, in cents per dollar of payout. 77c means you pay 77 cents and get 1 dollar back if it hits. It is also how likely the market thinks the block is.
  • Weather model: what the forecast alone says.
  • Honest odds: the model checked against the engine's own scored record. If past calls show the price was the better guide, the honest odds move toward the price (see how the engine works).
  • BET or READ: a call is a BET only when the honest odds beat the price by at least 3 points. Then the app and Your Engine show how to split a stake so every field pays the same. Everything else is a READ: the engine's view, posted and scored, with no stake suggested.

Most calls are READs. That is on purpose: the engine only says BET when its own record backs it up.

03How results are scored

Every call is saved with the time it went out, the block and the price. After the station reports, the result is posted, no matter how it went.

  • The free call of the day gets its result as a quote of the original post on X and as a reply under the call on Telegram. You always see the call and the result together.
  • Calls that were only in the app are collected once a day into one summary post: every city, every hit, every miss.
  • The running record in every result counts all calls since the current rules started on Sep 25, 2026.

The full list, with block, price and outcome for each call, is in the app under Record. Nothing gets deleted.

10How the engine works

The forecast

For every market the engine pulls ensemble forecasts from several global weather models (ICON, GFS and ECMWF) for the exact airport or station Polymarket settles on. Each ensemble member is one possible version of tomorrow. Together they give a spread of possible daily highs, which the engine turns into a probability for every temperature field. Near the end of the day it also folds in what the station has already measured, because the daily high can only go up.

Learning from its own misses

Every model runs a little warm or cold at some stations, and that changes with the seasons. Once a day the engine looks back at the last 21 days, compares its forecast with what each station really reported, and adjusts the correction for that city. A city needs enough results before its correction changes, so one odd day does not throw it off.

Honest odds

A forecast can be good and still add nothing to a price that already knows it. So once a day the engine checks its last 150 scored calls: did its own number predict the result better than the market price did? The answer sets how much weight the model gets. If the market was the better guide, the honest odds sit close to the price and calls go out as READs. If the model starts adding something the price did not have, its weight grows by itself and BETs come back.

It learns in public

When a correction or the model's weight changes, the engine posts what it changed and why, as "What I learned today".

When it stays quiet

No call goes out when the order book is too thin, the price is too high for the payout to make sense, the market disagrees with the engine by an extreme margin, or the station is one where the models are known to struggle. Most days that means only a few calls.

Your Engine never holds your money and has no access to your wallet. You buy on Polymarket yourself, or just follow along and it tracks how your picks would have gone.

Station Live

The markets settle on official weather stations, mostly at airports. Station Live shows what each of them has measured today: the high so far, the last reading and the local time. The day's high can only go up, so every bracket below it is already out, and the app crosses it off. Each call in the app also gets a line with what its station says right now.

Engine vs Crowd

Every market has a crowd favourite: the three brackets the market prices highest. For every market the engine locks that pick once, when it costs between 75 and 85 cents, and scores it after the day, right next to the engine's own calls. The Record tab shows both: hit rate, what the price said, and what a dollar on each would have done. If the crowd keeps winning, you will see it there first.

Lowest temperature

Polymarket also runs markets on how cold it gets: the lowest temperature of the day, at the same stations as the highs. The engine makes these calls the same way. It reads the forecast for the night and the morning, freezes its pick at local midnight and scores it against the station after the day. Calls and results go to the holder channel and the app, like the highs.

Kalshi, the second market

Kalshi runs daily high temperature markets too, for some US cities. The Kalshi tab in the app puts both prices next to each other for the same city and day. Where both settle on the same station, it is the same question, and a gap of 5 cents or more is marked. Elsewhere the stations differ (Kalshi uses Central Park for New York, Polymarket uses LaGuardia), so the prices can differ for a reason.

Every engine call and every crowd pick in a Kalshi city is also locked at the Kalshi price the moment it goes out, and scored on Kalshi's own result after the day. The Kalshi tab shows that record on its own: hit rate, what the price said, and what a dollar would have done, before Kalshi's trading fees.

Kalshi is a separate, US regulated exchange with its own account and rules. Check that it is available where you live. Your $ODDS tier works the same: the tab is part of the app.

Crypto Edition

The OddsEngine Crypto Edition lives at terminal.oddsengine.live. Polymarket runs Up or Down markets on BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, DOGE, BNB and HYPE in windows of five minutes, fifteen minutes and four hours. Up wins when the Chainlink average over the whole window ends at or above the price at the start.

That average is what makes these windows readable. Halfway through, half of it is already written. The engine takes the start price, the average so far, the price now, the time left and how much the coin is moving, and turns that into one number: the chance for Up. The terminal puts it next to the order book. When the engine sees more than the ask plus Polymarket's taker fee, the market gets an edge tag.

Five modules pick a side in every window and keep their own public record: Engine edge (the side the engine rates at least 5 cents above the ask after fees), Crypto lag (the coin moves hard on the exchange within a minute, confirmed over five minutes, and the Polymarket price has not caught up yet), Both sides (Up and Down together when both cost less than a dollar with fees), Cheap side (10 cents or less) and Late favourite (90 % or more on the engine, ask at 95 cents or less, near the end). Every entry uses the real ask and the real fee, ten shares each, held to the result.

At set points in every window the engine's number and the market price are written down and scored once the window settles, so you can see who called it better. These are tracked picks, not orders. You trade on Polymarket yourself.

12FAQ

Does the engine always win?

No. It misses regularly. That is why every result gets posted and the record is public. Judge it by the record, not by a single call.

What do BET and READ mean?

Every call shows three numbers: what the market price says, what the weather model says, and the honest odds. The honest odds are the model checked against our own scored record: if past calls show the price was the better guide, the honest odds move toward the price. A call is a BET only when the honest odds beat the price by at least 3 points. Everything else is a READ: the engine's view, posted and scored, but with no stake suggested. Your Engine sends only BETs unless you write /reads on.

Why are there days with no call?

Because most markets are priced about right. The engine only calls the few that are not. A day without a call is the engine doing its job.

Does the engine trade for me?

No. You place every trade yourself on Polymarket, from your own account. The engine and Your Engine only tell you what they see and size it for your stake. Your money never leaves your wallet. New to Polymarket? Section 09 and the Start tab in the app walk you through it.

What is coming next?

A new piece every few days. Each one is announced the day it switches on, and it shows up in the Updates section above the moment it does. Nothing is announced before it works.

Do I need $ODDS for the AI models?

You pay for the AI models with $ODDS: top up once, then every message is taken from your balance in dollars. Holding more $ODDS lowers your fee.

Does OddsEngine burn $ODDS?

Yes. When AI agents pay in USDC, our margin buys $ODDS and half of every buy is burned. Every buy and burn is listed with its transaction on public.oddsengine.live/burn.

Do I need $ODDS to use the app?

No. Without $ODDS you see one free call a day, every result and the full record. $ODDS unlocks every call live.

Is $ODDS an investment?

No. It is a key to a product. $ODDS is paired with $TORCH and has a 3% buy tax, set at launch: 1.05% of every buy goes to holders, paid out by the Torch contract based on how much and how long you hold, and claimed in the Torch terminal. 1.05% goes to the creator, 0.9% to Torch. OddsEngine itself pays nothing out, there is no staking, and nobody can promise you anything about the price.

Why Robinhood Chain?

It is where we launch, through Torch. And it is EVM, so the same wallet holds your $ODDS and trades on Polymarket.

Can I use Phantom?

Phantom can sign in, but it does not show Robinhood Chain balances. MetaMask or Rabby are the easier choice.

The app says my session expired.

Sessions last 7 days. Tap verify again and sign once more.

Where do I ask questions?

On Telegram or X. We never DM first and never ask for your recovery phrase.

13Risks

  • Prediction markets can lose you your whole stake. A call that misses pays nothing.
  • Past results do not predict future ones. Weather and markets change.
  • Prices on Polymarket move. The price in a call may be gone when you get there.
  • Weather stations can report late, report a different value than expected, or be corrected afterwards. The market settles on what Polymarket's rules say.
  • $ODDS can lose value. Only hold what you are fine with losing.
  • Smart contracts, bridges and wallets carry their own risks.
